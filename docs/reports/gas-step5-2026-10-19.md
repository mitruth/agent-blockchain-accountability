# Gas of the physical policies, as a function of the horizon k

Step 5's last acceptance criterion (spec Section 9): "gas is reported as a function of k".

**Measured on 19 October 2026** by `test/policies/Step5Acceptance.t.sol`
(`test_gas_k8` to `test_gas_k96`, `test_gas_dayAheadBatch`,
`test_gas_dayAheadBatchNoPolicy`), under Hardhat 3, Solidity 0.8.25, optimizer
200 runs.

## The setup

- **The submission.** The agent's whole submission, its session call included,
  on the ported P2P market through the gate, in steady state: a first order of
  each kind has been placed.
- **The market.** Hourly slots. The order is for a slot two hours ahead, so
  that even k = 8 covers it.
- **The battery.** home_01's as the honest arms ran it, half full, with its
  reading already held by the verifier. Handing in a reading with the order
  adds what `accept` costs: 87,468 gas the first time, less after (log 5.1).
- **What is measured.** Each line adds one policy to the ones above it. Every
  measured order is asserted to have been placed, not refused.

## The day-ahead case

A batch of 24 offers, one per hour of tomorrow, at k = 48:

| Policies | Gas |
|---|---:|
| None | 8,785,837 |
| A1–A6, S1, S2 export and import, S3, S4, S5 | 10,640,167 (+1,854,330, +21 %) |

S3 and S4 judge a batch once, over the whole horizon, whatever the number of
legs. S5 looks at each slot the batch touches.

## What the figures say

- **The cost of S3 and S4 grows with k, and k is set by the market.** In a
  day-ahead market, the horizon must reach the end of tomorrow: about 48
  hourly slots, or 72 to 96 half-hour slots.
  - At k = 48, the three physical policies add 405,000 gas to an offer.
  - At k = 96 they add 723,000, about twice the market's own cost.
- **S3 was made 42 % cheaper at step 5.4** (log 5.4). The projection runs in
  one unchecked function, and the projection without the order is skipped when
  the one with it is clean.
- **What remains,** about 4,000 gas a slot for S3, is the projection itself and
  reading k positions from the ledger.
- **S3 and S4 read the same positions and forecast** over the same horizon,
  each for itself. A combined policy would read them once. That is the
  monolithic variant spec D14 asks gas to be reported for, in step 6.
- **Where it can run.** A million gas per order is affordable only where gas
  is cheap, as on an L2. On Ethereum mainnet it would not be practical at
  k = 96. The paper should state the chain and its fees when it quotes these
  figures.

The figures are Hardhat's execution gas inside a test transaction. They include
the session call and the market; they exclude the transaction's base cost and
calldata.
